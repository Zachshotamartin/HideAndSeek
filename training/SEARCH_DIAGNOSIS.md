# Read-only diagnosis of room search

The sustained phase completed without changing its training rules. These probes explain candidate limitations; they neither train a replacement nor establish a cause for every failure. The exact frozen checkpoints, raw observations, and report hashes remain in `output/sustained-mixed/search-audit/` and `output/sustained-mixed/entity-failure-audit/`.

## Memory handling and delayed credit

The code and an actual adjacent-checkpoint audit agree: recurrent memory is not reset at a 32-tick chunk boundary. Optimization starts from the recorded pre-action GRU state, detaches its gradient, and advances it through the chunk. Episode termination resets both role memories and requested buttons before the next episode. Historical/current identities change only at those resets. Blind seeker preparation still advances memory, but its policy loss, entropy, and KL rows are masked. These paths also have independent numerical tests.

The audit compared actual sustained updates 224 and 225 on 32 independently initialized frozen diagnostic worlds. Reconstructing the old GRU from the complete recorded observation sequence matched the stored state exactly. Starting new-checkpoint chunks from old stored memory, rather than reconstructing the entire history with new parameters, produced a mean hidden-seeker distribution KL of 0.00023 (95th percentile 0.00112). That is measurable staleness, but it is small in this sample and does not establish a reset or burn-in defect.

The policy uses history beyond one chunk. Replaying only the last 32 observations from a zero state changed its hidden-seeker distribution by mean KL 0.0515; using 64 observations still gave 0.0128. At 128 observations it fell to 0.00023. This demonstrates dependence on history, **not that the memory contains a useful map or correctly remembers an occluded hider**. A larger GRU or longer BPTT cannot be justified solely by saying the present memory resets or forgets immediately.

Return credit is separate from BPTT. GAE spans the 256-tick rollout, with gamma 0.998 and lambda 0.98. A reward 80 ticks later retains 0.852 of its discounted-objective weight and 0.169 of its direct GAE residual weight. Preparation advantages are nonzero. Across the repeated episode/rollout alignment cycle, 240 of 1,280 preparation steps (18.75%) have their first play reward beyond the rollout boundary and therefore depend on the critic bootstrap. Early useful preparation actions can receive credit, although the critic’s uncertainty and long delays can make that credit noisy. Increasing BPTT alone would not change those scalar return weights.

For context, the research paper uses separate decentralized actors and a centralized critic, entity-aware attention, a 256-unit LSTM, and a 10-step BPTT window. Its return horizon is 160 steps with gamma 0.998 and lambda 0.95. Its successful large-scale training therefore does not support blaming our longer 32-step BPTT window by itself. Its batch sizes, model capacity, and experience scale are substantially larger than this project. We use the paper as a reference and have not copied its implementation or weights. [Baker et al., Sections 4–5 and Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/html/1909.07528v2#A2)

## Entity order sensitivity and its limits

The current first layer assigns different weights to the six distance-sorted object slots. In frozen recorded observations, swapping the same two visible records changed the seeker’s distribution by mean KL 0.144 and its deterministic movement vector by mean RMS 0.095. Even pairs within 5 cm of equal distance had mean KL 0.079, but that subset contained only 29 rows. These swaps preserve the unordered entity set, yet some are outside the native canonical sorting convention. They are representation probes, not direct estimates of lost reward.

A follow-up exactly replayed all 32 room maps from the sustained 8M assessment with the same unchanged 37M hider. Every replay reproduced its cached outcome. Among 20 complete search misses, 68.8% of play frames contained zero or one visible prop, and four complete misses had no persistent-entity slot changes at all. Among the 12 games that saw the hider, 86.6% of frames contained zero or one prop.

Persistent entities changed slots on 4.53% of failed-search frames versus 2.03% of successful-search frames. Pure rank swaps with an unchanged visible entity set occurred on only 0.34% versus 0.05%. Counterfactual slot-swap KL was similar when multiple objects were visible: 0.117 in failures and 0.111 in successes. Thus slot sensitivity is a plausible contributor in cluttered scenes; it does not explain all room failures, and these descriptive groups differ in trajectories and object exposure. No causal architecture claim follows from their association.

The probes made no optimizer updates. The first audit collected 16,384 frozen rollout decisions plus 5,760 diagnostic room decisions; the outcome-matched follow-up collected 15,360 decisions. They remain separate from both the sustained training budget and its milestone performance assessments.

## Conditional next-phase design

This is a design for consideration **after** the declared 32M assessment. It is not implemented or running. The goal is to remove a measured representation sensitivity while preserving the physical game, scoring, action semantics, and privacy boundary. It does not instruct agents to hide in a particular location, pursue a target, or manipulate any object.

Keep the same 140 actor inputs, 64-unit GRU, Gaussian movement heads, Keep/Press/Release tool controller, phase masking, original visibility reward, and frozen-opponent league. Changing only the entity encoder gives a cleaner comparison than simultaneously changing memory, action persistence, rewards, and episode length. Do not increase BPTT or alter lambda merely because rewards are delayed; the current audit does not identify those as the main defect.

The original replacement encoder would have two parts:

1. Preserve the existing linear contribution of self state, visible-opponent state, lidar, and requested buttons. Replace the six independent object kernels with one shared object kernel, applied to each permitted object record and summed. This removes slot-specific parameters.
2. Add a shared nonlinear object embedding and a small masked attention pool conditioned on the agent’s existing fixed-input representation. It has no slot index or positional embedding. Concatenate summed shared features with the attention result, then project a residual into the existing 96-value GRU input. A fixed null token makes empty sets finite and contributes zero when no entities are visible. All hidden entity payloads are masked before embedding.

All processing is body-relative and uses only the existing permitted measurements. Object kind, size, velocity, and ownership are already observed features; the network receives no new rule descriptions or goal-specific subgoals. The shared nonlinear embedding retains relationships within each object’s record, rather than reducing the scene to a sum of raw coordinates. Attention lets the policy choose relevant entities without assigning meaning to a slot number.

For an original warm start, initialize the shared linear object kernel by averaging the six existing slot kernels, retain the fixed-input weights and compatible GRU/action weights exactly, and initialize the new nonlinear residual output to zero. This is an explicit projection of our own learned parameters, not action imitation or an expert demonstration. **It does change the initial policy when objects are present.** It cannot preserve every canonical-slot behavior while also guaranteeing order invariance. When an entire observation history contains no visible objects, the preserved fixed branch and zero residual should reproduce the original policy exactly; test that property.

Start a fresh actor optimizer for this representation experiment and record the reset, rather than inventing historical Adam moments for newly shared or nonlinear parameters. Retain the original source checkpoints and all per-role lineage. A scratch reference uses the same new architecture with independently saved random weights and zero experiences; it must never be confused with the projected warm start. Measure both zero-update versions before allocating a long training run. Warm start is the practical candidate because it retains locomotion and visible-opponent processing; scratch is useful to detect harmful representation transfer, but reproducing the prior learning from zero will cost substantially more experience.

Keep the centralized critic separate. First assess its calibration under the changed actor representation and, if needed, fit it on complete fresh episodes without actor updates, using the established map-separated selection protocol. Count that value-only experience separately. No privileged scene state or fitted critic enters the actor export.

## Acceptance and deployment work for that design

Before any new optimization, test mathematical order invariance over all permutations of six slots on representative observations, with tolerances limited to floating-point summation error. Include full, partial, and empty visibility; invalid hidden payload perturbations; arbitrary object counts; persistent state across multiple timesteps; and identical seeded control outputs. Mask tests must verify that modifying hidden coordinates cannot alter logits or memory. Native physics and reward parity must remain unchanged.

After approval, benchmark the full rollout/update path before setting a meaningful fresh-interaction phase. Use a retained legacy control with the same documented optimizer-reset treatment when isolating the encoder effect. Keep identical world/opponent distributions, learning settings, active masks, and equal total interaction accounting. Compare each role against the current browser and strongest retained fixed opponents, including complete room-search misses. Broad validation remains the selection set; a separate untouched final cohort is used only after choosing a candidate. Useful tool claims still require paired interventions and actual physical traces, not attention patterns, grip counts, or a changed encoder.

Browser inference would use a new explicit actor-format identifier and content-addressed assets. Implement the small shared-embedding, mask, attention, and recurrent operations independently in JavaScript. Export only actor parameters and exact per-role provenance, with strict shape/finite checks. Extend the existing native/JavaScript multi-episode deterministic and sampled parity fixtures, including object permutations and empty sets. Keep legacy model import for comparison. The package’s entry stays lazy; model weights remain separate assets. The UI and replay controller continue to use the same physical observations and requested buttons. No public model asset changes before the chosen pair passes its measured comparison and the actual localhost seed-2709 follow-seeker review.
