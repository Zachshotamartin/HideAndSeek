# Retain the stronger seeker without replacing the hider

The matched 8M league arm improved its seeker against the same frozen 37M hider by 9.50 percentage points visible (36-map bootstrap 95% interval +2.08 to +17.69), while its hider regressed against the fixed initial seeker by 3.28 points hidden (−7.47 to −0.23). The whole league pair was not promoted. Its seeker weights remain a useful candidate.

The mixed candidate copies the **unchanged 37M hider** and **unchanged league B seeker after 8M additional interactions seeker**. It performs no averaging, fine-tuning, or policy update. Per-role source checkpoint SHA-256 and source-run interaction counters are retained. The two roles must not be described as having identical training lineage. Neither actor receives a centralized critic at inference.

Before further training or promotion, assess a fresh development cohort: 96 maps, 32 per scenario, seeds 1,500,140,000 plus scenario offsets of 1000. Arena sizes 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12 are cycled. Box counts 0–8 and ramp counts 0–2 are drawn independently from a fixed configuration RNG seed 913779. Both requested and actually placed object counts are recorded. Agent starts and complete geometry are generated by those fresh seeds. This covers the supported size/count range, with modest samples per subgroup; final 1.9B test seeds remain untouched.

The 14 conditions compare the mixed pair and original pair; both seekers against the identical original, zero-experience, persistent warm-start, and intermediate hiders; original and mixed hiders against the same initial seeker (identical weights, a consistency check); and the mixed pair with hider tools, seeker tools, or both disabled. Physical pushing remains enabled. The full league B pair is an additional reference. All paired conditions use the same role-specific sampled action tapes.

Primary question: does the retained seeker improve visibility against the same original hider across these new layouts without a credible regression against the other fixed hiders? Report whole-map paired 95% intervals, scenario/size/count breakdowns, and complete search misses. Useful tool strategies require separate causal outcome evidence and actual trace inspection; no promotion follows from more grabs, movement, or current/current scores. These opponents were available during self-play; the novelty is geometry and starts, not a claim of unseen-opponent generalization.

The outcome determines whether the mixed weights merit a longer, unchanged-reward self-play continuation. No new short training trial, reward shaping, scripted pursuit, forced object interaction, or altered physical semantics is part of this assessment.

## Recorded result

Across these 96 varied maps, the mixed seeker gained 2.45 percentage points of visibility against the unchanged 37M hider (paired 95% interval −2.91 to +7.90). The earlier narrow improvement did not establish a broad gain. Comparisons against the initial, warm-start, and intermediate hiders were also inconclusive, with no statistically clear regression. Complete search misses decreased from 25 to 22 games.

The hider’s grab/lock benefit was +1.11 points hidden (−1.21 to +3.66); the seeker’s was −1.26 points visible (−4.06 to +1.14). These results do not support a reliable useful-tool strategy. The mixed pair is retained as an initialization for longer self-play, with its exact per-role lineage. It is not promoted to the browser.
