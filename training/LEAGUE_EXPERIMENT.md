# Matched larger-rollout comparison

This original training experiment preserves the physical game, restricted recurrent actor observations, requested-state grab/lock controller, and unshaped zero-sum visibility reward. It does not change the development model shown in the browser. No demonstrations, forced tool actions, or tool-use bonuses enter either arm.

Both arms start from our exact 37,036,032-interaction persistent-button actor (`375fd147ae2af5c284b15e4c898342a5256db5295907e302b530da0b61763075`) and the accepted separately fitted residual critic. Actor optimizer state is preserved, with the learning rate explicitly reduced to .0001. Critic warm-up used 1,966,080 environment decisions, reported separately from this trial. The critic receives privileged scene state only during training; exported actor inputs remain the same 140 restricted observations.

Arm A uses current hider versus current seeker. Arm B samples an opponent assignment independently at each complete episode: 50% current/current, 25% current hider/historical seeker, and 25% historical hider/current seeker. Historical actors are frozen copies of our persistent warm start (22,937,600 prior decisions), intermediate pilot (29,229,056), and development parent (37,036,032). Selection is uniform within that pool. No opponent identity enters actor observations.

Settings are matched: 128 environments, 8 worker processes, 256 control ticks per rollout, recurrent chunks of 32 ticks, minibatches of 256 chunks, two PPO epochs, actor/critic learning rates .0001, entropy coefficient .005, and active-row KL cutoff .008. Only current-policy samples enter actor losses, advantage normalization, entropy, and KL. Blind seeker preparation samples are also excluded from those objectives while memory still advances. Frozen-opponent samples may train the separate value model. All-frozen minibatches skip the actor optimizer completely, including momentum updates.

Each arm receives 7,995,392 total actor decisions across 122 complete rollouts. Both arms together use 15,990,784 new training decisions, counting historical actors as well as current actors. Checkpoints at 3,997,696 and 7,995,392 decisions are assessed. Per-role current-policy decisions, historical decisions, and active policy samples are recorded separately; a league arm therefore has fewer trainable samples at an equal physical interaction budget. Evaluation interactions are separately labeled and never used for optimization.

The first complete rollout in each arm doubles as the throughput benchmark and remains inside its training budget. Both arms then restart processes at the same boundary, restoring model/optimizer/RNG states but starting fresh worlds and zero memory/button states. This restart is explicit in provenance, not described as exact uninterrupted rollout continuation. The same world and opponent-assignment RNG schedules are used by both arms. Training worlds use seeds below 2**30. Source snapshots and checkpoint hashes are retained for each process segment.

Validation is fixed in advance: 12 maps per scenario (`shelter`, `rooms`, `open`), seeds starting at 1,500,110,000 plus scenario offsets of 1000, at 8m with three boxes and one ramp. Both checkpoints and both arms use the same maps and role-specific action tapes. This is a narrow development comparison, not evidence over every supported size or object count; final-test seeds at 1.9B remain untouched. Compare each trained role against the exact frozen parent and genuine zero-experience initial opponent, with the corresponding unchanged opponent-pair baseline. Evaluate both full-tool and role-specific grab/lock-disabled rollouts; pushing remains physical in these ablations. Direct A/B cross-play provides additional comparison, not a replacement for fixed opponents.

A negative role change whose paired-map 95% interval excludes zero rejects that candidate for promotion. An inconclusive result does not establish noninferiority or useful tools. More grabbing, longer holds, prop movement, or current/current win rates cannot qualify a strategy. Useful interaction claims require paired outcome evidence and review of actual trajectories. Both assessed checkpoints and negative results are retained; the browser's 37M development pair is the rollback model throughout the trial.

Implementation checks cover current/frozen row accounting, actual actor identity and pre-action memory in grouped inference, blind preparation controls, frozen-to-current episode reset gradient isolation, and skipping all-frozen Adam steps even with invalid off-policy likelihoods. The independent read-only review found two provenance omissions (critic minibatch resume lock and helper source snapshots); both were corrected before continuation.

The first-rollout benchmark measured A at 65,536 decisions in 6.37s (4.88s rollout, 1.46s optimization) and B in 7.56s (6.06s rollout, 1.47s optimization). These are local throughput measurements, not performance claims about the learned behavior.

## Completed result

Both arms completed their declared 7,995,392 interactions, for 15,990,784 total. Arm A collected 3,997,696 current-policy decisions per role. Arm B collected 3,007,152 current hider decisions and 2,992,448 current seeker decisions; its remaining 1,995,792 decisions came from frozen opponents. The active hider/seeker loss samples were 3,997,696 / 2,662,400 for A and 3,007,152 / 1,992,960 for B. Each arm completed 16,640 games. Both checkpoint stages and negative results are retained.

Neither 4M pair qualified because its hider regressed against the genuine initial seeker. At 8M, the historical-opponent seeker gained 9.50 percentage points of visibility against the fixed parent hider (paired 95% interval +2.08 to +17.69), but its hider lost 3.28 points hidden against the initial seeker (−7.47 to −0.23). The whole pair remained rejected. Arm A did not show a statistically clear role improvement. Neither arm established a useful grab/lock advantage. Longer holds and larger prop movements were not treated as success.

The unchanged 37M hider and B seeker were retained as a separate mixed candidate. Its broader 96-map assessment is recorded in [`MIXED_ROLE_ASSESSMENT.md`](MIXED_ROLE_ASSESSMENT.md). It did not confirm a broad seeker gain, although no fixed-opponent regression was established. The longer continuation protocol is separate in [`SUSTAINED_SELF_PLAY.md`](SUSTAINED_SELF_PLAY.md).

Compact measured reports are included in [`reports/league-4m.json`](reports/league-4m.json) and [`reports/league-8m.json`](reports/league-8m.json). Full local reports are `output/league-trial/validation-{4m,8m}/evaluation.json`, alongside frozen checkpoints, source snapshots, native trace pairs, and gradient diagnostics. Each milestone used 792 evaluation games (380,160 actor decisions) without optimizer updates. Read-only diagnostics confirmed replayed action likelihoods within 9.54e−7, unchanged actor weights, and no evidence that tool entropy gradients dominated reward gradients. Median grips remained 0.16–0.24 seconds; trace review showed route changes and both beneficial and harmful incidental contacts rather than a reliable construction strategy.
